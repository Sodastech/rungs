title: FAQ
description: Common questions about Rungs, its scope and its status.

## Can I use Rungs today?

Not from npm yet: the first release is 0.1. You can try it from source (see
[Getting started](/rungs/getting-started/)) and watch the
[repository](https://github.com/Sodastech/rungs) for releases. Until 1.0, the API may still change;
every change is recorded in the changelog.

## How is it different from a workflow engine such as Camunda or Temporal?

Rungs does one thing: approvals. It is a library that runs inside your Node.js application, on
your database, with no server to operate and no diagram language. In return it handles the
approval-specific cases those engines leave to you: requesters who are also approvers,
delegation without double votes, steps that end up with nobody to approve, four eyes, return and
resubmit. See the [edge case catalog](/rungs/edge-cases/).

## Does it include a user interface?

No. Rungs gives you the data (inbox, timeline, previews, lists) and you build the screens. The
[example app](https://github.com/Sodastech/rungs/tree/main/examples/nextjs-expense) shows one way.
Ready-made React components are planned.

## Which databases does it support?

PostgreSQL 15 or newer through `@rungsjs/store-postgres`, with node-postgres, postgres.js or
PGlite, and an in-memory store for tests. Other databases need a store adapter; the
[store contract](/rungs/reference/store-contract/) describes what it must do.

## Does it know my org chart?

Not by itself. Approvers come from your application through `roles` and resolvers you write, for
example "the requester's manager" from your user table. Resolvers that understand organization
structure (department heads, acting holders of vacant positions) are planned.

## Who may approve, withdraw or administer?

Rungs checks routing: whether an actor has a task on a request, or is its requester. It does not
authenticate anyone or decide who is an administrator. Your application does that before calling
a command.

## Can it run on serverless platforms or the edge?

On serverless Node.js runtimes, yes, with a PostgreSQL connection pool; run `tick` from a
scheduled function. Not on edge runtimes, which cannot open database connections the store needs.

## What happens to requests in progress when I change a flow?

Nothing. Each request keeps the flow version it was submitted with
([EC-37](/rungs/edge-cases/#ec-37)); new requests use the new version.

## Business days and holidays?

Durations are absolute time by default. Pass a `calendar` to `createRungs` to count business days
with your own holidays ([EC-30](/rungs/edge-cases/#ec-30)).

## Where do I report a bug or ask a question?

In the [GitHub issues](https://github.com/Sodastech/rungs/issues). For a bug, the playground
scenario that shows it is the most useful report.
