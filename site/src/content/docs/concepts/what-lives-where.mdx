Rungs is not where your business data lives. Keep the record itself (an expense with its lines
and receipts, a leave request, a purchase order) in your own tables, and point Rungs at it with
the request's `subject`. Put in the `payload` only what the flow's conditions read.

<Snippet file="concepts/host-data.ts" region="link" />

| Data | Kept by |
| --- | --- |
| The record: title, lines, receipts, everything your users edit | Your application |
| The approval: status, steps, tasks, decisions, audit trail | Rungs |
| The link | `subject: { type, id }` on the request |
| The payload: what conditions read | Rungs, as a snapshot at submit |
| Users, roles, who is active | Your application, through callbacks |

**Why the payload is not the place for business data.** It is a snapshot taken at submit and
does not follow later edits ([EC-36](/rungs/edge-cases/#ec-36)); it is copied into every list
result; and it may hold personal data.

## Showing lists

Ask Rungs for the approvals and load your records for that page in one query:

<Snippet file="concepts/host-data.ts" region="list" />

`rungs.requests()` filters by requester, status, subject or flow; `rungs.inbox()` lists a user's
tasks; `rungs.blocked()` lists steps waiting for an admin. Rungs does not check who may see a list:
show requesters their own requests, approvers their own inbox, and the blocked list to admins.

The [example app](https://github.com/Sodastech/rungs/tree/main/examples/nextjs-expense) follows
this pattern.
